# *Anything Sharp Facing Down* — Critic's Report and Revision Log

## 1. The verdict

This is a serious novel and a rare one. It has one subject: a woman whose whole moral life runs through precision, meaning what she counts, what she leaves out and what she doesn't say. The form carries that subject as well as the sentences do. There are two books, and the published one is a lie told in the correct format. The drawer copy is the truth, and it still stalls at the one place where truth matters. The ending lands three times over. The seam in Chapter 17 (the offering, not the failures) is the best single move in the book. The husband's reversed anecdote is the cruellest. The last line, in another hand, takes the book out of the narrator's control at the only moment it could bear it.

The book's weakness is not ambition. It is **damage done by its own later revisions**. The density pass that took the draft from about 42,500 to about 55,000 words left seams: a paragraph printed twice, a name given to two different people, scenes inserted into the middle of other scenes, and above all **numbers that are false**. In most novels a wrong date is a typo. In this one it is a character flaw the author never meant to give her. The canon says it outright: *"Her arithmetic is her character."* A narrator who opens Chapter 8 by telling us she has counted words since she was nine cannot be caught saying sixteen words when there are seventeen. A prize jury would not forgive that, and neither would the first sharp reviewer.

So this pass was not a rewrite. It was a forensic repair. I read every line against every other line, every date against a calendar, every age against her birth year, and every claim about the book against the book. I fixed what was broken and touched nothing that was working. The canon's warning stands: past a certain point, each new pass adds as many defects as it removes. This pass removes defects only.

Single novels do not win the Nobel; bodies of work do. What a single novel can do is be flawless in its own terms, and after this pass this one is much closer.

## 2. What changed — the full log

Each change states the problem and the evidence. Dialogue quotation marks were also normalised throughout (see §2.12). Chapter 7's protection order was respected: it received two copyedits of fact and nothing else.

### 2.1 Errors in the book's central numbers

| Ch. | Was | Now | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | "It's not inconsistent with the underlying condition **at this stage**." | "…**at present**." | The line as written is **17 words**. The book, the canon and the brief all call it the sixteen-word answer, and "Sixteen was cheap" is the book's anchor. Now exactly 16. |
| 14 | "A range from a number to six times the number." | "…to **more than** six times the number." | 4→25 is 6.25×; the rhyming range in Ch. 9 (90→600) is 6.67×. Neither is "six times". Now true of both, which tightens the rhyme. |
| 14 | "Fourteen words, **in the passive**" | "…**in the first person plural**" | *"We have not seen the material…"* is active voice. "First person plural" is accurate, and it quietly prepares Ch. 15's condolence "ruined by *we*". |

### 2.2 Continuity breaks from the density pass

- **Ch. 8: duplicated paragraph.** "I did it in the mornings before the building filled up…" appeared twice in a row, as an expanded version and the original. Merged into one, keeping the security-light texture and the Mr Odubote line.
- **Ch. 2: two Whitlows.** Whitlow is the editor who takes her to lunch, and "Anders Whitlow" was also the photographer she "met for eleven minutes". The photographer is now **Anders Holm**.
- **Ch. 2: the title passage was out of order.** "I let it go the way I let things go" sat two paragraphs after the thing it refers to, with the lunch in between. Now the order is:
  1. The three titles she let go.
  2. "I let it go… completely."
  3. **"Except once."** The fourth title, the one on our cover.

  The secret held-out title now arrives as a catch on her own claim to let everything go, which is the book's method in miniature.
- **Ch. 2: the photograph.** She told the photographer she thought of "the morning room", but at fifty she lived in Cheam. The morning room belongs to the Rutland house after 2025. She now thinks of **the room over the boiler at Nottingham**. The reader doesn't yet know what that is, and Ch. 3 makes it the happiest place of her life. So the face on the published jacket is the face of a secret joy nobody can read, which is the whole book.
- **Ch. 3: Frank Loder's letter appeared before Frank Loder.** The paragraph sat in the Aylmer section, ahead of "Frank Loder ran the section". It also said "five years on", which matches no event. It now follows "I said I thought that was fair. And it was." and reads "eight years on, in 1987". *Knew I was right* now points back at "You're a very difficult person to be certain about", and her "better of him and worse, in strict rotation" finally has both meanings.
- **Ch. 3: Dr Rennard was introduced twice.** He is named in the second paragraph, then reintroduced as the anonymous "man from another department… whose name was Dr Rennard". He is now named as the course tutor ("lent to us from mathematics"), and the later "whose name was" is gone.
- **Ch. 6: the honeymoon interrupted the wedding lunch.** The Mrs Askey paragraph ended with "There were speeches, badly… my father cried", which belongs to the Marchmont lunch. The block now runs in order:
  1. Morning ("Well.").
  2. Lunch and speeches.
  3. Terry Isard's photographs.
  4. "Well, he's very sound."
  5. Bridget's tea towel.
  6. Wensleydale and Mrs Askey.
  7. Home to Cheam.
- **Ch. 6: the Cheam house interrupted the courtship.** It sat between "together about eighteen months" and "He was in Nottingham and I was in London and it was the train". That had the neighbours taking them for a settled couple while they were still long-distance. It now follows the honeymoon.
- **Ch. 9: Mr Prentice took over the verdict on the article.** "It is fair to everybody, including us" had been pushed into the newsagent digression and seemed to describe the village. It is back with the article it judges.
- **Ch. 14: the statement scene was scrambled.** "He came up afterwards" came before he had finished speaking, and "That was the sixteenth of May, in the afternoon" followed a paragraph about Saturday, Sunday and Wednesday. The order is now:
  1. Statement.
  2. He sits down.
  3. Denise: "That's that, then, madam."
  4. He comes up.
  5. "That was the sixteenth of May."
  6. The days of papers, ending on the bus shelter.
- **Ch. 14: the withheld name leaked.** Mrs Enderby's quotation began *"Ryan wasn't led astray"*, one page before *"I have not written his name… my hand declined"*, and a chapter before Ch. 15 claims *"I have not written it in fourteen chapters."* It now reads *"He wasn't led astray."* The device holds.

### 2.3 The morning of the fourteenth (Ch. 12, checked against Chs. 3, 14 and 17)

Four passages disagreed about the most important morning in the book:

- Ch. 12 had her **entering** at 6:35. But at 6:20 Aled finds her already holding the cutlery basket (confirmed in Ch. 3), and Ch. 14 says **he** came down at 6:35.
- Ch. 12 had Martin load and run the machine **at 11:20 the night before**. Ch. 17's seam rests on him loading it **at ten past five, in the dark, while she slept**. The offering only exists if the second version is true. Ch. 12's "hot plastic and lemon" also implies a cycle that has just finished.

Repairs:
- "I came in at about **a quarter past six**."
- On Monday night he "stacked the plates by the sink, which is as far as he ever got at night".
- "The machine had been run **in the small hours** and not emptied."
- The radio "had been moved along the counter since the night before, which meant he had already been in and tried it." She reads evidence as she always does, and the careful reader gets a clue to Ch. 17.
- "That **happened in the middle of** all of the above, and I have put it after."

Now every clock in the book agrees: up at four; machine at 5:10; her entry at 6:15; Aled at 6:20; the folder from 6:20 to 6:35; Martin in the kitchen at 6:35; "twenty to seven"; the car at 7:08; the signature at 8:10.

### 2.6 Dates and places that can't both be true

- **Ch. 3: the paper and the corridor.** The paper "came out in the autumn of **1977**" and she reads the offprint outside Aylmer's door, but she went to Cattermole in autumn 1976. Publication is now **autumn 1976**. The corridor scene happens in her last weeks there, and everything downstream holds.
- **Ch. 6: the kitchen scene.** "It was the autumn of **1991**… I had come back from the committee that had taken the liver work… Rosalind had put my name on it twice." That committee is Trevanide, **June 1986** (Ch. 5), so it is now the **summer of 1986**, and "thirty-five years" becomes "**forty**".
- **Ch. 17: no constituency yet.** In September 1996 Martin "was in the constituency four days of the seven", but he entered Parliament at 49 in 1997. Now: "**in Nottingham**". He was still at the county.
- **Ch. 4: one son in two places.** "The other stayed local", then a paragraph later "they lived in Bristol and in Hamburg". Now: "**is in Bristol**."
- **Ch. 8: who was in the meeting.** "There were ten of us" at the meeting, but she then fears speaking "in front of **eleven** people". Now **nine**, meaning the others in the room. This also removes one eleven.
- **Ch. 8: Bob's postcards.** Nine summers of postcards "until the building was emptied", but Nine Elms emptied about 2003, two summers after Bob retired. The row now moves to the new kettle and "stayed up".
- **Ch. 7 (copyedit only):** Christine's second child is a boy ("They had two boys"), so "holding **her**" becomes "holding **him**". And "the **flat** at Cheam" becomes "the **house** at Cheam", to match Ch. 6's first house.

### 2.7 The pill strip (Ch. 2)

The text said Tuesday's sort covers Wednesday and Thursday and Friday's covers the weekend and Monday, and that "the arithmetic has never once failed". As written, Tuesday and Friday were covered by nothing. She takes her pills at five, before Karen comes, so it now reads **Tuesday's covers Wednesday to Friday; Friday's covers the weekend and the Monday and the Tuesday**. Now the arithmetic really has never failed.

### 2.8 The decision-verb recital (Ch. 16)

The text said she had used the verb "on my own age, and on **my mother's question**, and on a coincidence of counties". No "I have decided" in the book touches her mother's question. Ch. 2's third use is *"I have decided not to find out"*, about Martin not asking to read the book. Now: "on **my husband's silence**". *(Canon §12's list, "the prognosis", is also wrong; the text is now right.)*

### 2.9 Claims about this book

The canon's rule is total: every number she gives about this book must be true.
- Ch. 6: "a sentence I have been circling for **four hundred pages**" (we are about 90 pages in) becomes "circling **since the yellow house**".
- Ch. 16: "describing for four hundred pages" becomes "**for fifteen chapters**".
- Ch. 17: "led by the hand for four hundred pages" becomes "**for sixteen chapters**".

These match the house style already used in Chs. 14 and 15 ("for fourteen chapters"). Ch. 2's "Four hundred pages" stays: it is the planned memoir, not this book.
- Ch. 15: "the spring is **two chapters away**, and I find I want one more ordinary chapter before it". The spring is in the next chapter, so it now reads "**I find I want one ordinary thing before it**", and Hannah's section, which opens Ch. 16, is that thing.
- Ch. 16: "There it was. **Fifty** years" becomes "**Sixty** years", matching Ch. 2's count of a life in which nobody asked ("Sixty years. Parents, brother…").

- "…and was happy there in my fashion~~, which by now has a chapter of its own~~."

Ch. 14: a double space in "WHEN —  and" is fixed.

### 2.12 Quotation marks (brief item 5)

All dialogue and quotations in the manuscript (Part 2 only) are now **single quotation marks, UK style**. Before converting, I checked every line for quotes nested inside quotes and found none. The one line that mixed both styles, Ch. 5's committee corridor with Wesley and Rosalind, was resolved. Apostrophes are untouched. Parts 1 and 3 (brief and canon) are unchanged.

## 3. Deliberately not changed

- **Ch. 2's lie** ("nothing to be clear about" in the 5–8am hours). It is the designed catchable lie.
- **Ch. 7 beyond the two copyedits.** It is under a protection order.
- **Martin's numbers.** "Nineteen years" (Ch. 4) and "Forty years" (Ch. 18) stay: he is right about dates and loose about durations, by design.
- **The tea "thirty-one years"** (Ch. 2 and Ch. 18, "since 1995"). It is consistent and dated, so it is a deliberate unexplained point.
- **"The fence, for it was at bottom about the fence"** (Ch. 18). It reads as an intended joke tying the surveyor saga to Colin's fence in Ch. 11.
- **The walk-on cast and the wit.** I recommend an editor-led trim (§5) but did not make one. The canon defends these on purpose, and cutting by my taste rather than the author's would be the overreach the canon warns against.

## 4. Questions only the author can answer

These are ambiguities, not clear errors, so I left them:

1. **How long Josie has worked with her.** Ch. 13 says "four months"; Ch. 16 (late May, a later scene) says "three months"; Ch. 18's rubber band is "four months old" from Ch. 13's Thursday. All three can't be true. Decide when sessions began (March, per the proem and Ch. 18) and adjust Ch. 13.
2. **"Christine came within a sentence of it in a kitchen in 1979"** (Ch. 16). No such scene exists. That's fine if it's meant as untold, but every other name in that list refers back to a scene we've read.
3. **"A sentence I have now written about two different courts in two different decades"** (Ch. 14). I can find only one failure on standing. Is the first one missing?
4. **The sharps bin "there for ten of" twelve years** (Ch. 7). Injections appear from IVF in 1992, which gives about five years. Protected chapter; your call.
5. **"Her council, her town"** (Ch. 15). Ryan is from Sleaford (North Kesteven) and the room is in Grantham (South Kesteven). Is Lorna now in Grantham, or should this read "her county"?
6. **Commuting.** Martin works for Nottinghamshire county council until 1997, yet lives in Cheam from 1984 and gets home "at seven" from Sheffield. One clause would settle it, perhaps a weekday flat in Nottingham.
7. **"Ninety pence and one sentence away"** (Ch. 4). The ninety pence is unexplained. If it's a phone call that wasn't made, the kitchen scene doesn't support it.

## 5. Recommendations for an acquiring editor (not acted on)

1. **Trim about 3,000–5,000 words from the walk-ons.** The density pass gave a dozen minor characters the same closing tail: a lovely last line, then "and I have wondered since…". "For completeness" appears four times and "I notice" sixteen. Each one is good on its own; together they become a mannerism, and the minor characters start to sound like the same paragraph. Cut a third of them and the rest shine. Candidates: the Ch. 5 induction rules; the chief executive's "customer"; Íñigo; Mr Prentice.
2. **Keep the length argument in the canon, not in the meeting.** The novel's force is compression. If an editor asks for more, the answer is no. If an editor asks for less, listen.
3. **Do not reopen the seam (Ch. 17) or the coda (Ch. 18).** They are finished.
4. **Before submission, update canon §10 (age 70 → 71) and §12 (the decision-verb list)** so the canon matches the text again.
