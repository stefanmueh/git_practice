# *Anything Sharp Facing Down*: Editorial Report on the Final Edition

## 1. Verdict

This novel is built with real seriousness. Its subject is a woman whose moral life runs entirely through precision: what she counts, what she leaves out, and what she won't say. The form carries that subject. There are two books: the published memoir, a lie told in the correct format, and the drawer record, the truth, which still stalls at the one place where truth matters. The best single move is the seam in Chapter 17 (the offering, not the failures). The cruellest is the husband's reversed anecdote. The last line is in another hand.

What was holding the book back was not its ambition but **damage from its own revisions**. In a novel where the narrator's arithmetic is her character, every false number reads as a flaw the author never meant to give her. There were false word counts, dates that can't both be true, a paragraph printed twice, two people with one name, scenes inserted into other scenes, and promises the book never kept. This edition repairs all of that and makes a measured set of line-level improvements. Every change was checked against the rest of the book before it went in.

**One limit a line edit cannot fix.** An independent reader put it like this: Alison is almost always ahead of the reader, glossing patterns before we can find them. The danger is that the Chapter 17 seam reads as one more lucid confession rather than a rupture. This edition thins her glosses and the parade of wise, quotable walk-ons, and that helps. The rest is the book's chosen design: a narrator who knows everything she reveals except the one thing she could never name. An editor may want to push further. I would push gently.

## 2. How this edition was made

1. **Forensic read.** I read every line against every other line, every date against a perpetual calendar, every age against her birth year (early 1953), and every claim about the book against the book.
2. **Second pass.** I resolved the open questions from the first read, repaired two unpaid narrative debts, and retired several verbal tics.
3. **Independent continuity audit.** A second reader checked every number, list, weekday and age. I verified each finding and accepted almost all of them.
4. **Independent critical line edit.** A third reader proposed about 50 line-level changes. I judged each one, accepted most, rejected those the canon designs (§4), and in places applied a gentler version than proposed.

The brief and canon (Parts 1 and 3 of the handoff file) are updated to match the text. A new canon §16 records this read.

### 3.3 Seams from the density pass, closed

- **Ch. 8:** the paragraph printed twice is merged into one.
- **Ch. 2:** the photographer and the editor were both called Whitlow. The photographer is now Anders Holm.
- **Ch. 2:** the title passage now reaches its secret as "**Except once**". The cover photograph shows her thinking of **the room over the boiler at Nottingham**, a plant that Chapter 3 pays off.
- **Ch. 3:** Frank Loder's letter now comes after Frank Loder, and Rennard is introduced once.
- **Ch. 6:** wedding, honeymoon and home to Cheam now run in order.
- **Ch. 9:** the verdict on the article ("It is fair to everybody, including us") is back with the article, not swallowed by the newsagent digression.
- **Ch. 14:** the statement scene is in sequence.
- **Ch. 14:** "Ryan" no longer leaks a chapter early.

### 3.4 Promises kept

- **Ch. 5 → Ch. 14.** "There is one more number of this kind in this book… we will come to it." The canon claimed this was paid; it wasn't. It now is, in her register and without a figure: *"On the yellow cards, below the level, nobody looks. Here, below the level, nobody has to stop."*
- **Ch. 2 → Ch. 13.** Josie's indifference to politics is "restful", and "I will come to it." It is paid at the breakfast question: *"She did not need the job. She only needed the breakfast."*
- **Ch. 11.** The first of the two times she saw "Wait. Go back." is now kept back on purpose rather than lost.

### 3.5 Contradictions inside single sentences

- **Ch. 2:** "I cannot make those two sentences into one" sat inside a single sentence. They are now two sentences.
- **Ch. 2:** Hannah has "one theory of me, it is wrong, and she has never needed another", not "no theory at all".
- **Ch. 9:** "Not filling silences is my own first language" is cut. Chapter 13 says the opposite, and so does the scene it sits in.
- **Ch. 6:** Martin's opinion on milk quotas "he has held ever since". He is alive.
- **Ch. 6:** "The entire mother-of-the-bride record" is cut, because her mother speaks again in the ladies.
- **Ch. 2:** "I told it before the soup" is cut. The lie was told over coffee.
- **Ch. 17:** "A method with no name on it" now reads "**without my name** on it". It has two names on it.
- **Ch. 14:** Mrs Enderby's sentence is now "the reverse" of a margin note. On the cards, margins went nowhere; out of doors, her sentence goes everywhere.
- **Ch. 16:** "for once did not sit down", "the extract was quoted everywhere", and Christine's approach now points at her real question from this spring.

### 3.6 Fewer tics, fewer interruptions

- **Walk-ons that broke a climax, cut:** Bob Lycett's postcards (in the middle of the six-day countdown), Íñigo (who pre-empted Rosalind's four sentences), and the *Cumbria confirmed* card (which split a scene).
- **Glosses retired:**
  - "for completeness", in all four places;
  - the announced Cansdale echo;
  - the book naming its own thesis in Chapter 8;
  - "the moral progress of this entire book";
  - the re-explained Waddington callback;
  - the Chapter 3 passage duplicated in Chapter 6;
  - four "I have wondered since…" tails;
  - "where titles go to die";
  - "read a room";
  - two Americanisms ("toward").
- **The record's last sentence.** The surveyor clause now ends at "the lad knew his stuff", so the final sentence runs clean to the blue cup.

**Chapter 7.** The protection order held: four copyedits of fact ("him", "house", "drove up", and the sharps bin's five years), nothing else.

## 4. Rejected, deliberately

- **"I have stopped estimating"** (Ch. 3). The canon designed it as the narrator renouncing her own habit.
- **"Somebody bringing the car round"** (Ch. 16). The canon names it as the end of Pat's war.
- **"A small thing or the largest thing there is"** (Ch. 11). It is the book's central question about Martin.
- **The repeated "most truthful person"** (Ch. 18). The irony of the repetition is the point.
- **Martin's loose durations** ("Nineteen years", "Forty years"). By design, he is right about dates and wrong about durations.
- **The Chapter 2 lie about the 5–8am hours.** It is the book's one catchable lie.
- **No new scenes, characters or facts**, beyond the two debts the book had already promised to pay.
